Global Market Overview

The 2026 industry is not collapsing. It is sorting itself.

Volume is under pressure in mature markets, but growth persists where products solve a new occasion: affordable social beer, flavor-led RTDs, non-alcoholic beer, selective premium moments and digital convenience.

Demand pressure

Consumers are budgeting harder

High living costs, energy-price volatility, tariffs and softer on-trade visits are pushing shoppers toward value, smaller packs and fewer categories per occasion.

Category upside

RTD and no-alcohol keep recruiting

RTDs were the only major category with positive 2025 growth in IWSR's preliminary read. No-alcohol beer grew strongly by both volume and value.

Premiumization 2.0

Premium has to justify itself

The old broad trade-up curve has stalled. Winning premium propositions now need provenance, convenience, lower-risk trial sizes and clear occasion value.

Demographic split

Asia grows, Europe ages, the US moderates

South Asia and East Asia-Pacific carry the strongest macro growth. Europe faces aging and low GDP growth. The US remains wealthy but increasingly health-aware.

Regional Intelligence

Four regions, four strategic jobs to be done.

The platform divides the market into the United States, South America, Europe and Asia. Each panel links macro context to category behavior and the best commercial moves.

United States

High purchasing power, high moderation pressure
Macro

Still attractive, but consumer confidence is uneven

  • GDP: IMF projects US real GDP growth at 2.4% in 2026.
  • Consumer health turn: Gallup reports US adult drinking fell to 54%, a near 90-year low.
  • Income split: NIQ shows higher-income households still pull premium segments while lower-income cohorts are cutting back.
Category Opportunity

Premium beer, spirits RTD and no-alcohol

  • RTD: NIQ places RTDs above 12% of total alcohol dollars.
  • Non-alc: Non-alcohol beer, wine and spirits surpassed $1B in sales, acting as a complement rather than only a substitute.
  • Beer leaders: Constellation remains the leading US high-end beer supplier by dollar sales.
Go-to-market

Recommended 2026 moves

  • Defend premium with affordability: smaller packs, variety packs and trusted brands beat unsupported price escalation.
  • Use RTDs as the recruitment platform: spirits-based cocktails, lower sugar and flavor discovery should sit beside core beer and spirits portfolios.
  • Build compliant DTC and retail media: use no-alcohol variants, wine DTC learnings and state-specific data capture to deepen first-party insight.
  • Segment by income and generation: premium cues for $100K+ households, value multipacks and credible moderation claims for pressure-sensitive shoppers.

Best-positioned companies

Constellation Brands for high-end imported beer, Modelo, Corona and Pacifico. AB InBev for scale, Michelob Ultra, Cutwater and digital B2B. Diageo and Brown-Forman for premium spirits, whiskey and cocktail adjacency.

Risk: health perception, lower drinking frequency, Hispanic-consumer pressure, aluminum/tariff cost and state-by-state ecommerce constraints.

South America

Urban scale with value pressure and digital route-to-market upside
Macro

Moderate growth, high urban concentration

  • GDP: World Bank projects Latin America and the Caribbean growth at 2.3% in 2026.
  • Urbanization: Latin America is among the world's most urbanized regions, supporting dense distribution and last-mile beverage delivery.
  • Brazil: IWSR reported 2025 TBA volume down 4% and value down 3%, so growth must be taken through share, occasions and better execution.
Category Opportunity

Beer, premium beer, low-sugar RTD and no-alcohol beer

  • Beer base: Brazil remains the anchor market, but weak weather and consumers hurt 2025 beer volumes.
  • No-alcohol: AB InBev reported strong growth in Brazil no-alcohol beer, a useful hedge against moderation.
  • Digital: B2B ordering and cold-beverage delivery can lift availability and reduce stock-outs in fragmented retail.
Go-to-market

Recommended 2026 moves

  • Returnable and value architecture: protect volume through affordable packs without abandoning premium ladders.
  • Urban cold-chain advantage: prioritize delivery-ready beer, RTD and no-alcohol bundles for Sao Paulo, Rio, Bogota, Santiago and Buenos Aires.
  • Local flavors and events: tailor RTD, cider and beer-adjacent launches to carnival, football, beach and music occasions.
  • Distributor digitization: replicate marketplace logic from leading brewers to strengthen independent retail execution.

Best-positioned companies

AB InBev/Ambev for Brazilian beer scale, no-alcohol beer, BEES and delivery assets. Heineken for premium beer challenger positioning. Diageo, Pernod Ricard and Campari for premium spirits, aperitifs and cocktail culture.

Risk: currency volatility, affordability pressure, excise changes, inflation and climate/weather swings that hit beer demand.

Europe

Mature, aging and regulation-aware, but rich in premium occasions
Macro

Low growth and energy sensitivity

  • GDP: IMF projects euro-area growth at 1.1% in 2026 amid energy shock and elevated risks.
  • Demographics: Europe is structurally older, with many countries already past population peak.
  • On-trade: cost-sensitive consumers are more selective about going out, pressing beer, wine and spirits volumes.
Category Opportunity

No/low beer, aperitivo, tourism and premium brands

  • Heineken: 2025 total volume declined 1.2%, but Heineken brand volume grew 2.7%.
  • Carlsberg: alcohol-free brews grew 4% and premium beer grew 5% organically in 2025.
  • Campari: organic growth of 2.4% underscores the resilience of aperitif-led portfolios.
Go-to-market

Recommended 2026 moves

  • Make moderation social: no-alcohol beer, aperitif spritzes, low-ABV cocktails and adult soft drinks should be integrated into the same social occasions.
  • Protect margin through mix and productivity: invest behind global premium brands, but simplify operations and pack architecture.
  • Use travel retail and tourism: airport, rail and destination-commerce bundles can offset weak domestic growth.
  • Prepare for health policy: transparent labeling, responsible marketing and low-sugar variants reduce regulatory risk.

Best-positioned companies

Heineken and Carlsberg for beer, non-alcohol and portfolio productivity. Pernod Ricard and Diageo for premium spirits. Campari for aperitivo, tourism and cocktail occasions.

Risk: energy costs, low population growth, stricter alcohol-health messaging, fragmented regulation and value pressure in on-trade.

Asia

The largest long-run growth field, split between aging North Asia and young South Asia
Macro

Growth is strongest where population and incomes are still expanding

  • GDP: World Bank projects 2026 growth of 4.4% in East Asia-Pacific and 6.2% in South Asia.
  • Urbanization: UN projections place much future urban-population growth in Asia, especially India and China.
  • Demographic split: China and Japan are aging, while India, Indonesia, the Philippines and Vietnam still offer younger consumer growth.
Category Opportunity

India growth, Japan RTD, China reset

  • India: IWSR preliminary data showed 2025 TBA volume up 4% and value up 5%.
  • China: 2025 TBA value dropped 12%, driven by national spirits, making baijiu modernization urgent.
  • Japan: RTD momentum and beer-adjacent innovation fit aging, convenience-led lifestyles.
Go-to-market

Recommended 2026 moves

  • Overweight India and Southeast Asia: build state-by-state partnerships, local production and tier-2 city distribution.
  • Reframe premium in China: move from status gifting toward lighter occasions, cocktails, lower-proof formats and younger adult discovery.
  • Scale RTD learning from Japan: convenience retail, low-sugar, seasonal flavors and premium cans can travel across the region.
  • Use local heroes: global brands need local flavors, local social rituals and stronger language-market creative.

Best-positioned companies

Asahi and Suntory for Japanese beer, RTD and no/low innovation. Carlsberg for China and India premium beer growth. Diageo and Pernod Ricard for whisky, agave and premium spirits in urban Asia.

Risk: China national-spirit contraction, regulation, excise complexity in India, currency swings and local competitor speed.

Consumer And Demographic Trends

The consumer is becoming more intentional, not simply less social.

The next growth cycle belongs to portfolios designed around moments, health cues, affordability, convenience and local culture.

01

Gen Z and younger millennials are selective drinkers.

IWSR research shows Gen Z participation has stabilized, but category repertoires per occasion narrowed from 2.8 to 1.8 over two years. That points to fewer, more deliberate choices rather than a full category exit.

02

Moderation is now a mainstream purchase behavior.

Gallup's US data, NIQ's non-alcohol sales threshold and IWSR's no-alcohol beer growth all point in the same direction: moderation is becoming part of regular alcohol category planning.

03

Premiumization is shifting from status to proof of value.

Consumers still pay up, but they ask why. Smaller formats, provenance, clear ingredients, bartender credibility, limited releases and gifting do more work than broad shelf-price increases.

04

Urbanization supports convenience-led distribution.

Asia and Latin America concentrate large consumer pools in dense urban areas, making cold delivery, on-demand bundles, convenience-store RTD launches and B2B retailer apps structurally valuable.

Long-Term Strategic Plan

A practical 2026-2030 growth plan for beverage alcohol leaders.

The plan assumes flat global TBA in 2026, recovery only in selected pools and continuing pressure from health, affordability and regulation.

2026: Stabilize the core and remove avoidable friction.

Simplify underperforming SKUs, protect core beer and spirits availability, rebuild trust with distributors, use revenue management by pack and occasion, and build a monthly market-signal dashboard for tariffs, energy, consumption and category shares.

2027: Scale the growth engines.

Expand RTD, no-alcohol beer, low-sugar formats and urban delivery bundles. Shift innovation funding from novelty-only launches to products that recruit repeat occasions and can survive tighter shelf space.

2028: Rebalance the geographic portfolio.

Increase growth capex in India, Southeast Asia and high-density South American cities. In Europe, invest selectively in non-alcohol, aperitivo and tourism. In the US, favor premium beer, RTD and better-for-you spirits claims.

2029-2030: Become beverage-occasion companies, not only alcohol companies.

Use data, retail media, non-alcohol extensions, adult soft drinks and responsible drinking platforms to own social occasions across ABV levels, while keeping premium alcohol brands distinctive and scarce enough to hold margin.

Portfolio

Run a barbell strategy

Protect accessible core volume with value packs and returnables, while reserving premium investment for brands with clear differentiation and repeatable occasions.

Innovation

Build RTD and no-low as platforms

Prioritize spirits-based RTD, premium canned cocktails, non-alcohol beer, low-sugar variants and regional flavor systems over one-off launches.

Channels

Digitize the route to market

Use B2B ordering, retailer data, compliant DTC, delivery partnerships and retail media to improve availability, measure occasions and reduce promotion waste.

Risk

Plan for regulation and health pressure

Prepare transparent labeling, responsible marketing controls, moderation-friendly portfolios and scenario plans for tax, shipping and advertising changes.

Talent

Hire local category builders

India, Brazil, China and Japan require people who understand excise, local flavor codes, channel economics and platform commerce.

Capital

Allocate by growth quality

Use a hurdle that combines GDP growth, legal-drinking-age population, urban density, premium headroom, digital maturity and regulatory clarity.
